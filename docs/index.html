The thesis

Inbound won't fill a round. You build the list, find the paths in, and keep every thread moving in parallel — without dropping one.

And once you're in conversations, the operational signals matter as much as the pitch: how fast you follow up, how specific your outreach is, how ready your data room is the moment someone asks. Run the skills when you need them; each uses your shared project files and adapts to a quiet, passive raise or a sharp, time-boxed process.

The problem

Most founders raise by improvising. That's the wrong lever.

A raise is a parallel operation with a dozen live threads, each decaying if you miss the next action. Here's where it leaks.

✗ NO LIST

Waiting on inbound

You take the meetings that come instead of building the 40 funds whose thesis actually fits. The round never gets a real top of funnel.

✗ NO PATHS

Cold into the void

You email partners with no warm path mapped. A trusted intro beats ten perfect cold emails — but only if you find it first.

✗ GENERIC

Mail-merge outreach

The same email with the name swapped. A partner smells it in one line. Specificity is the difference between ignored and answered.

✗ DROPPED

Threads fall through

Twelve conversations, twelve next-actions, no system. The follow-up that comes three days late tells the investor how you'll run the company.

✗ UNREADY

Scrambled data room

A partner asks for the room and you spend two days assembling it. Readiness on the spot is itself a signal — absence of it is too.

✗ GHOSTED

Silence after a "no"

You go quiet on the funds that passed. They're often the leads of your next round — if they watch you execute for six months.

The 15 skills

Fifteen specialists. One source of truth. All free.

An on-demand workflow for your raise: invoke a skill in Claude Code or Codex to prepare materials, update the project files, and surface the decisions that need you. Scheduling and external integrations are configured separately.

Decide

Phase 00 · whether & when
raise-decision
Decision Engine

Should you raise at all?

Each founder answers a short questionnaire independently — readiness, dilution tolerance, time appetite. It surfaces where you disagree, runs the dilution math and the alternatives, and returns a verdict with teeth: raise now, not yet (with the milestone that flips it), or don't raise.

Foundation

Phase 01 · context & strategy
raise-context
Context Engine

The single source of truth

Interviews you, pressure-tests every claim, and flags what's missing or weak before it ever reaches an investor. Everything downstream inherits this file.

fundraising-plan
Raise Strategist

Passive vs. active vs. hybrid

Picks up the verdict from raise-decision and sets the mode — a quiet "always slightly raising" or a sharp, time-boxed process — writing the intensity into a plan file every downstream skill reads.

Build

Phase 02 · list, paths, story, model
investor-list
Target Builder

The 40 funds worth your time

Ranked by fit, with evidence coverage and eligibility shown separately. See which partners match, which need more research, and which conflicts rule out outreach.

warm-intro-map
Path Finder

The best path to each partner

Maps the strongest warm intro through your network and drafts the forwardable note your connector can send in fifteen seconds.

deck
Narrative Architect

Story first, slides second

Headlines that are claims, not labels. A traction page partners screenshot. Creates a deck with your app’s available tools, or returns clearly labelled slide content.

financial-projection
Model Builder

Defensible, not fantastical

A driver-based model plus the one-page assumptions memo you actually defend. Runway and ask reconcile exactly with the deck.

pitch-rehearsal
Pitch Coach

Practice the hard questions

Rehearse your opening, face skeptical investor questions one at a time, and tighten answers around evidence. Unknowns become preparation tasks.

Run

Phase 03 · reach, manage, anticipate
investor-brief
Meeting Preparation

Walk in prepared

A sourced brief on the fund, partner and relationship, with tailored talking points, likely objections, questions to ask and a proposed next step.

outreach
Message Writer

Written for one partner

A researched hook, one proof point, a small specific ask — never the same body with the name swapped. Warm and cold variants per target.

pipeline
Process Manager

Nothing drops

A weekly Monday brief of what needs action, same-day follow-up generators, and stage tracking across every thread in parallel. Speed is the message.

data-room
Diligence Anticipator

Answers before they're asked

The full index, a claim-to-proof map, a ranked gap list, and staged access. Ready the moment a partner asks — which is itself the signal.

Sustain & Close

Phase 04 · warmth, tempo & the wire
investor-comms
Warmth Engine

Two tracks — yes and no

Monthly update drafts when you run the skill, with an honest challenges line. Out-execute the reason they passed, in writing, and the next round writes itself.

pace
Chief of Staff

Keeps the machine in sync

Decides which skill runs next and why, keeps the plan, sheet, and pipeline files consistent with each other, and protects founder attention. The glue that orchestrates the whole raise.

closing
Close Driver

The terms that actually matter

A plain-language term-sheet readout — pool placement, preference, board — negotiation prep, and a real close window. Clarifies so you decide; not legal advice.

FAQ

Common questions

Starting a raise? Follow the six-week plan, use the daily and weekly check-ins, or copy the Claude chat prompt sequence.

I want to close in six weeks. What should I do each week? +

Treat six weeks as a target to test against evidence. This is a suggested operating plan for a prepared early-stage raise, not a promise or an industry conversion benchmark. Warm relationships, investor decision calendars, your instrument and legal readiness determine what fits. If you are starting cold or need an institutional lead, the date may need to move. Use existing work and enter at your actual stage.

Define the finish line first: the full target amount received, or an explicitly agreed first close with its own amount. A positive meeting, verbal interest, term sheet and signed investment documents are different milestones; none should be reported as cash received. Put a real start date, target funding date, amount, currency, raise lead and company jurisdiction in fundraising-plan.md. Ask counsel and prospective investors early whether the timetable is workable.

Begin with the copyable kickoff request, then use the daily and weekly pacing prompts throughout the six weeks.

Week 1 — Get ready and open the first conversations

  1. Days 1–2: use raise-decision to record or confirm the team's decision, raise-context to reconcile facts, and fundraising-plan to set the active process, dates, ownership and fallback. Define the milestone the money funds and who protects company operations.
  2. Days 2–4: use deck and financial-projection for a defensible story, ask and cash plan. Use data-room to identify the essential evidence and assign missing items. Get counsel started on corporate, cap-table and instrument readiness now.
  3. Days 3–5: use investor-list and warm-intro-map to research a manageable target set. Around 30–40 qualified candidates is a planning starting point for an active process, not a quota. Separate likely leads from followers; exclude blocked targets and finish research before treating uncertain fit as qualified.
  4. Practice with pitch-rehearsal. Once the essential materials are credible, use outreach to draft a small first wave, for example 3–6 suitable investors. Review and send through your normal channel, then record actual sends in pipeline. Avoid spending a whole week polishing cosmetics.

Friday checkpoint: consistent context, usable deck/model, core diligence evidence, a researched first wave and named owners. Some introductions or meetings should be in motion; if key facts or documents are missing, name the blocker and revise launch dates.

Week 2 — Run parallel first meetings

Refine the story from the first conversations, then approach the strongest qualified targets promptly. Use a short calibration wave to learn; do not hold your best investors back until the final weeks. Batch meeting slots to protect preparation and company work. Before each meeting run investor-brief; rehearse the hardest objection with pitch-rehearsal. Ask who decides, whether they can lead, their likely ticket, what evidence they need and their actual decision timetable.

After every meeting, supply factual notes to pipeline, draft the promised follow-up and send it when ready. Record the next step, owner and date, distinguishing a proposal from an investor-confirmed commitment. Follow agreed dates; do not send daily reminders merely because you check the pipeline daily.

Friday checkpoint: track substantive replies, completed meetings and dated next steps. Have second meetings or specific requests emerged? If meetings are not materializing, fix targeting, introduction paths or the message before expanding volume.

Week 3 — Turn interest into diligence and a decision path

Prioritize investors taking concrete next steps: bringing another decision-maker, requesting relevant diligence, or agreeing a decision meeting. Use data-room to close evidence gaps and prepare appropriate access; you still control actual sharing. Reconcile every answer with raise-context and the model. Keep additional qualified conversations moving while the strongest prospects evaluate.

For a lead-dependent round, identify at least one plausible lead with an explicit process and dates. For a round that does not require a lead, track investor-by-investor decision and funding dates instead. Use closing to prepare questions and a checklist as terms emerge; use investor-comms for useful, factual updates to active investors when there is real news.

Friday checkpoint: a credible path to decisions should exist. No follow-on meetings, diligence or named decision timetable means the six-week target is at risk. Run pace and re-plan now, not in week 6.

Week 4 — Seek decisions and align the closing timetable

Ask advanced investors what remains for a decision and arrange the relevant partner or decision-maker conversations. Use closing to compare proposed terms and prepare negotiations with counsel. Confirm amounts, conditions and each investor's process. Keep verbal interest, written indications and signed documents separate.

For a priced, lead-dependent round, use this week as a planning checkpoint for lead/terms progress and an agreed diligence/legal timetable. Counsel may require more time; this is not a universal two-week closing rule. For rolling investments, process each eligible close on the applicable documents and conditions. Respect any agreed exclusivity or other obligations.

Friday checkpoint: there should be a specific route from the current status to funding: outstanding decisions, diligence items, documents, approvals, owners and dates. Without that route, mark the deadline red and decide whether to extend it or pursue a feasible first close.

Week 5 — Resolve the remaining conditions

Run closing with the current documents and counsel's updates. Maintain an investor-by-investor checklist for remaining diligence, allocations, approvals, legal documents, signatures and expected funding dates. Resolve critical questions promptly; agree a delivery date when an answer needs work. Follow up with interested participants using real progress and genuine deadlines.

Keep a short daily blocker review with the responsible people. Confirm the administrative steps with counsel, investors and your finance owner. A spreadsheet of hoped-for checks is not a funded round.

Friday checkpoint: every amount needed for the planned close has a documented status, owner and expected funding date. Identify any gap explicitly; do not count unsigned or conditional amounts as received.

Week 6 — Complete the close and reconcile the money

Work through the remaining approvals, documents, signatures and funding conditions with counsel. Track expected transfers and verify actual receipts against the company account with your finance owner. Use pipeline to keep signed and received amounts distinct. Close out the cap-table and document records with the responsible professionals, then use investor-comms to draft the appropriate investor update.

Finish-line checkpoint: report the amount actually received versus the original target, remaining commitments and conditions, and the resulting cash plan. If only a first close is complete, call it a first close and set the next milestone. Do not silently redefine a partially funded round as the full target achieved.

The week labels above are our planning recommendation. Background reading: YC on parallel investor conversations and YC's seed fundraising guide. Instrument and closing mechanics should be confirmed for your company with counsel.

01 · raise-decision — Decide whether to raise +

When: Week 1, or confirm an existing decision. Keep: raise-decision.md.

Help us decide whether to raise now, later, or not at all. Ask which founders are involved and collect each founder's view on readiness, dilution, time commitment, what capital unlocks and the alternative without funding. Surface disagreements without inventing consensus. Use supplied cash, runway and traction; ask for missing facts. Compare relevant funding alternatives and show dilution calculations with explicit assumptions. Give a reasoned recommendation for us to decide, measurable readiness gates if needed, and a revisit date. Return a dated raise-decision.md draft.
02 · raise-context — Build the company context +

When: Week 1; refresh when facts change. Keep: raise-context.md.

Build our fundraising source of truth from the supplied files and answers. Interview me in batches of 3–5 questions about the company, founders, product, customer problem, traction and metric definitions, business model, market, competitors, differentiation, raise amount/instrument, use of funds, cash/runway, ideal investors, each founder's network and communication style. Separate actuals, forecasts and assumptions. Flag contradictory figures and mark unknowns TBD. Return a dated raise-context.md draft plus green/yellow/red readiness gaps and the most important issue to fix before outreach.
03 · fundraising-plan — Set the six-week plan +

When: Week 1; revisit at decision gates. Keep: fundraising-plan.md.

Using our agreed raise decision and current context, plan a six-week raise. Ask for the start date, funding deadline, amount, stage, jurisdiction, current investor conversations, possible lead, raise owner and weekly capacity. Confirm whether close means first money received or the full round funded. Assess whether active, passive or hybrid fits; do not promise the deadline. Produce weekly milestones, owners, evidence needed, investor decision and legal dependencies, outreach waves, daily/weekly reviews and fallback decisions. Separate planned from investor-confirmed dates. Return fundraising-plan.md; surface any conflict with our decision or runway.
04 · deck — Prepare the investor story +

When: Week 1; revise from real feedback. Keep: deck.md.

Using our current context and plan, draft a concise investor pitch with a clear problem, customer, solution, why now, traction, business model, market, competition, team and funding ask tied to milestones. Give each slide a claim-based headline, supporting evidence, proposed visual and speaker notes. Cite the supplied source for material claims; flag missing proof and conflicting numbers. Keep forecasts labelled and remove invented urgency or certainty. Return deck.md. If I request slides, use available file tools and verify the result; otherwise provide slide-ready content without claiming a PowerPoint exists.
05 · financial-projection — Build the financial case +

When: Week 1; update with actuals. Keep: financial-assumptions.md and model.

Build a driver-based fundraising model from our supplied actuals. Ask for missing cash, revenue drivers, costs, hiring plans and funding assumptions before calculating. Model 18–24 months of monthly revenue, costs, cash and runway, with base and downside cases. Separate actuals, forecasts and editable assumptions; reconcile the ask, use of funds and milestone with the deck. Show formula logic, key sensitivities and a one-page financial-assumptions.md memo. If spreadsheet tools are available, create a formula-based workbook and verify its calculations; otherwise give tables/CSV and a formula map, clearly labelled as a fallback.
06 · investor-list — Research and rank investors +

When: Week 1; expand selectively. Keep: investors.tsv and investor-scoring.md.

Build or rescore investor targets for our agreed stage, sector, geography, desired individual check size and lead requirement. Size the list to our plan; do not pad it. Research funds AND partners using available browsing, citing sources and dates; without browsing, use supplied evidence and label it unverified. Gate documented mandate mismatches and direct competitor conflicts before ranking. Score known dimensions 0, 0.5 or 1 with weights: sector 25, stage 20, geography/check size/lead behavior/portfolio relevance/recent activity 10 each, partner relevance 5. Report fit = earned weight / known weight and coverage = known weight / applicable weight, both as percentages. Unknown is not zero or clearance; a sector-only match is 100 fit at 25% coverage and needs research. Exclude genuinely inapplicable dimensions from both denominators; if no evidence is known, report fit as unknown. Missing mandatory fit or conflict evidence means research needed, not eligible. Explain eligibility and research gaps. Return investors.tsv and investor-scoring.md, preserving existing IDs, notes and columns; suggest a qualified first wave.
07 · warm-intro-map — Find introduction paths +

When: Week 1; before each outreach wave. Keep: Warm-intro map and draft notes.

Using our investor list and each founder's supplied network, identify one best introduction path per qualified target and a backup if supported. Name the relationship owner and label each path verified, unverified or cold. Do not infer a real relationship from a shared employer or social connection alone. Hold blocked targets out of recommended outreach. Draft a short founder-to-connector request and a forwardable investor introduction grounded in what the connector actually knows. List relationships we must confirm and targets needing cold outreach. Return the map and proposed investor-sheet updates; preserve other columns and send nothing.
08 · data-room — Prepare diligence evidence +

When: Week 1; deepen during weeks 3–5. Keep: Data-room index and gap list.

Review our supplied company materials and prepare an investor data-room index. Cover corporate/cap-table records, financing, financials, metrics, product, customers/contracts, team, intellectual property and other relevant diligence. Map material deck claims to actual supporting documents. For every missing or stale item, show its importance, owner and proposed due date. Recommend staged access appropriate to the investor's progress and flag sensitive material for review. Return an index, claim-to-proof map and ranked gap list. Do not claim a room exists, upload files, change access or share anything.
09 · pitch-rehearsal — Practice the pitch +

When: Before outreach and important meetings. Keep: Feedback and rehearsal notes.

Run an interactive investor pitch rehearsal using our supplied context and deck. Start by inviting my two-minute opening, then wait for me. Give focused feedback on clarity, evidence and the ask, correcting unsupported claims against the source context. Role-play a skeptical but constructive investor and ask one question at a time. Wait for each answer before critiquing it and suggesting a tighter, factual outline. Do not invent my answers or assess voice/body language from text. At the end, summarize what we actually practiced, evidence gaps and next practice priorities. If I request a preparation pack instead, label it preparation, not a completed rehearsal.
10 · outreach — Draft the first approach +

When: Weeks 1–2; later qualified waves. Keep: Unsent partner-specific drafts.

Draft personalized outreach for the qualified investors I identify, using our context, investor evidence and introduction map. Confirm the sender/relationship owner and whether each approach is warm or cold. Use one specific, sourced reason for this partner, one verified proof point and a small concrete ask. Flag research gaps or portfolio conflicts rather than inventing personalization. Include the subject, email, any connector note needed and a proposed follow-up date consistent with our plan. Avoid fabricated introductions, endorsements, attachments or competing offers. Label all messages unsent; do not send or change last-contact dates.
11 · investor-brief — Prepare for a specific meeting +

When: Before every investor meeting. Keep: investor-brief-[fund].md.

Prepare a concise brief for [fund / partner] using the supplied company context, investor research, relationship notes and meeting details. Separate confirmed details from proposed times. Summarize fund and partner fit with sources/dates, relevant investments, conflicts, unknowns, relationship owner, last actual contact and open promises. Do not attribute a firm investment personally to a partner without evidence. Give three tailored talking points, likely objections with honest answer outlines, questions to ask and a proposed next step. Flag deck/context discrepancies. Return investor-brief-[fund].md; do not claim calendar/CRM access, book anything or change pipeline stage.
12 · pipeline — Keep every investor thread current +

When: Daily and after every interaction. Keep: pipeline.md and follow-up drafts.

Update our investor pipeline from the latest supplied table and factual interaction notes. If either is missing, ask for it. Preserve investor IDs, relationship owners, stages, actual last-contact dates and agreed follow-up dates unless new evidence changes them. Track sourced, intro requested, contacted, meeting, diligence, partner meeting, term sheet, closed and passed as appropriate. Show due/overdue actions with owner and date; exclude passed/closed threads from live chasing. Separate verbal interest, signed amounts and received money; unknown amounts stay unknown. Return pipeline.md, today's three priorities and requested unsent follow-up drafts. Drafting is not contact; do not invent inbox sync or sent messages.
13 · pace — Keep the six-week process on track +

When: Monday/Friday; whenever a milestone slips. Keep: Pacing review and pacing-log.md entry.

Review our dated fundraising plan, context and latest pipeline as of [today]. Compare this week's milestones with actual evidence and assess the funding deadline as on track, at risk, off track or unknown. Show days remaining, stale/missing updates, the main blocker, the next skills to run and three priorities with owners/dates. Check consistency across the deck, model and pipeline, and protect time for company operations. Look two weeks ahead at decision, diligence, legal and funding dependencies. Recommend continue, adjust or re-plan; never silently move the deadline. Return a dated entry for pacing-log.md. Do not claim reminders are scheduled.
14 · investor-comms — Keep investors informed +

When: When there is useful news; monthly thereafter. Keep: Unsent investor updates.

Using the supplied current metrics, prior update and pipeline, draft separate concise updates for live/committed investors and investors who passed or asked to stay informed. Include a one-line summary, consistent metrics versus the prior period, concrete wins, an honest challenge, specific asks and the next milestone. Explain any changed metric definition and label missing comparisons. Respect communication preferences and never imply a passed investor has re-engaged without evidence. Keep verbal, signed and funded statuses distinct. Give suggested recipients and the relationship owner, but label everything unsent and do not publish or change contact dates.
15 · closing — Get from terms to funds received +

When: Prepare early; use as terms emerge, especially weeks 4–6. Keep: Terms comparison and closing checklist.

Using the supplied proposed terms, cap table, plan and current investor statuses, prepare a closing brief for our decisions with counsel. Explain valuation/cap, dilution assumptions, option pool, preferences, control, investor rights and other material clauses present. Flag jurisdiction-dependent or unclear points; do not invent missing terms or tell us what to sign. Compare supported alternatives and identify negotiation priorities. Build an investor-by-investor checklist for decisions, diligence, approvals, documents, signatures and expected funding, with owners and dates. Keep proposed, agreed, signed and received amounts distinct; verify the close against the stated first-close/full-round target. Do not announce a close or initiate transfers.

Keep using the six-week milestones and progress scorecard. Browsing, file creation and scheduled tasks depend on the tools available in your chat; copying a prompt does not enable those capabilities.

How do I keep pace every day and every week? +

Use one owner, one plan and one pipeline. The raise lead owns the process. Every investor thread has a named relationship owner. Keep using the same project files in either app and finish one app's edits before switching.

  • Each morning — 15 minutes: provide yesterday's real interactions and use pipeline for today's due actions, investor promises and overdue threads. Choose three actions that advance decisions. Respect existing follow-up dates.
  • Before each meeting — 15–20 minutes: use investor-brief, then rehearse one difficult question. Know the next step you want to agree.
  • After each meeting — 10 minutes: record questions, objections, requested material, actual contact date and proposed/agreed next step. Review the follow-up draft and send through an authorized channel.
  • Monday — 30 minutes: use pace to compare this week's milestone with the evidence, choose the next skills, assign owners and protect time for running the company.
  • Wednesday — 20 minutes: unblock the most important stalled decision or missing diligence item. Escalate to the person who can resolve it.
  • Friday — 30 minutes: compare plan versus actual, review the next two weeks, and choose: continue, change the approach, or re-plan the deadline. Save the decision and reason.

These are suggested review slots, in addition to research, meetings and follow-through. Choose times that fit your workload; scheduling a review does not make the underlying work disappear.

Which numbers tell me whether the six-week close is on track? +

Use a small scorecard with this week's plan, actual, change since last review, owner and next date. Count unique investor firms consistently and separate lead-capable firms from followers. Track:

  • Qualified targets, actual approaches sent, substantive replies and first meetings completed.
  • Second/partner meetings, active diligence and explicit decision dates. A booked meeting is not a completed meeting.
  • Open investor requests and founder promises, their due dates and blockers.
  • Amounts at each distinct status: verbal interest, written indication/term sheet, signed investment documents, and cash received. Do not sum these statuses as if they were separate checks; track each investor's amount once per status view.
  • Cash received against the close target, unfunded gap, current runway and remaining time. Unknown amounts stay unknown. Use consistent currencies or explicit dated exchange assumptions.

Green: milestone evidence exists and the remaining decisions, documents and funding steps fit the confirmed timetable. Amber: an important step slipped or timing depends on an unconfirmed decision. Red: the required decision path is absent or a critical step cannot fit the remaining time. Unknown: updates are too stale or incomplete to assess. These are working assessments, not probabilities of success.

Agree activity targets from your actual starting pipeline and founder capacity. Review conversion using observed counts and comparable periods; do not treat a tiny sample as a forecast or assume a fixed number of emails guarantees funding. A week full of meetings without agreed next steps can still be off track.

What if I am behind in week 3 or still have no lead in week 4? +

Run pace immediately with the latest facts. Diagnose where progress stops: no replies suggests a targeting, access or message problem; first meetings with no next step suggest an unresolved fit or evidence issue; diligence without decisions calls for a clearer process, owner and date. Ask investors for specific feedback instead of guessing their motives.

If your round requires a lead and none has a credible decision path by week 3, mark the deadline at risk. If week 4 ends without actionable terms progress and a workable legal/funding timetable, treat a full close by week 6 as off track unless concrete evidence supports an exception. Those are planning escalation points, not universal fundraising laws.

Use fundraising-plan to compare an extended process, a feasible first close, a revised scope or a pause to improve evidence. Discuss financing changes with counsel and revisit the cash plan using actual runway. A smaller first close only helps if it funds a useful next milestone; it must not quietly replace the original target. If the round does not need a lead, assess signed participation and funding dates instead.

Record the decision, owner and revisit date. Do not manufacture competing offers, claim signatures you do not have, or impose a fictional investor deadline to rescue the calendar.

What's the difference between passive and active fundraising? +

Passive is "always slightly raising": a rolling instrument kept quietly open, warm inbound only, founder stays building — lower intensity, no public in-market signal, but weaker competitive tension. Active is a structured, time-boxed process: full list, parallel conversations, an engineered close window — better terms and speed, but it consumes the founder and a stalled process is visible. Hybrid — passive posture with active bursts — is the most common reality. The fundraising-plan skill makes that call deliberately, and then configures every other skill to match the mode you chose.

Can it tell us NOT to raise? +

Yes — that's the point of raise-decision. Its three outcomes are raise now, not yet (with the specific milestones that flip the answer and a revisit date), and don't raise (with what to do instead — revenue, angels, debt, or staying bootstrapped). No path silently defaults to "raise".
