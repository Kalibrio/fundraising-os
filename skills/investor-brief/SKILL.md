# Investor Brief

Make the next investor conversation specific, factual, and useful. Prepare a
brief the founder can scan immediately before the meeting.

## Inputs and scope

Read the available `raise-context.md`, `investors.tsv`, `pipeline.md`, and deck
from the user's project, plus supplied meeting details and notes. Honor the
user's instructions and existing decisions. If the investor is ambiguous, ask
which meeting; if other details are missing, label them unknown and proceed.
Do not require the whole fundraising workflow to be completed for one brief.

Use available web research for current public fund and partner facts. Calendar
and CRM are optional sources when connected and authorized; supplied notes are
sufficient. With no browsing, identify the supplied sources and their dates,
and label current deployment, role, and thesis claims unverified. Never invent
meetings, commitments, introductions, personal interests, or citations.

## Build the brief

1. Establish the meeting's status, date, timezone, attendees and objective from
   evidence. Distinguish confirmed from proposed. Preserve the relationship
   owner's name, last actual interaction and outstanding promises. A draft or
   this preparation session is not a new interaction.
2. Research the firm and the specific partner: stage, geography, check size,
   lead/follow behavior, relevant investments, public thesis and recent activity.
   Record source links or supplied document locations and evidence dates.
   Do not infer a partner personally led a deal from the firm's participation.
   An old fund announcement does not prove capital is available today.
3. Explain fit and material unknowns. Flag direct portfolio conflicts before
   proposing disclosure. Separate sourced facts from your interpretation and
   suggested questions. Use professional relevance for personalization; do not
   manufacture rapport or research irrelevant personal characteristics.
4. Choose two or three talking points from the company's actual evidence, tailored
   to this investor. Where the deck and context disagree, show the discrepancy
   and use the explicitly authoritative/current source; do not silently change
   facts to make a stronger pitch. Label targets and forecasts as such.
5. Prepare likely objections with honest answer outlines and evidence still
   needed. Include questions that resolve real uncertainties about fit, process,
   ownership, check size or timing. Propose a concrete next step appropriate to
   the meeting's stage, without presenting it as agreed.

## Deliver

Save `investor-brief-<fund-or-partner>.md` in the user's project with:
- Meeting details and desired outcome; confirmed versus proposed status.
- A short firm/partner profile, fit, conflicts and evidence gaps.
- Relationship history and promises, attributed to their actual sources.
- Tailored talking points, likely objections and questions to ask.
- Proposed next step, owner and follow-up preparation.
- Sources, dates and unresolved discrepancies.

Update an existing brief for the same meeting without erasing user notes; use a
meeting date in the filename when separate meetings need separate briefs. In a
chat-only session, return the brief inline or as a download and do not claim a
file was saved. Keep output out of installed skill directories.

Preparing a brief does not book a meeting, send a message, share documents or
advance pipeline stage. Perform those actions only within the user's explicit
authorization and available capabilities. Once a meeting has actually happened,
route its supplied outcomes to `pipeline` if that skill is available.
